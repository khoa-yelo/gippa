Peptides outside annotated genes define candidate open reading frames in seven classes; four get a class FDR: intergenic, out-of-frame in a gene, antisense, and N-terminal extension. Novel peptides have a lower prior than annotated ones, so error is estimated within each class and never pooled: database peptides compete with class-matched decoy ORFs, de novo peptides use their genome decoy hits. An ORF is called when its class q-value is at most 0.01 by default and meets its class rules. In the other classes, or a class whose FDR cannot be estimated, an ORF needs at least two distinct peptides and carries no FDR. For de novo ORFs the ORF q-value is the smallest peptide q-value, which can understate ORF-level error.
