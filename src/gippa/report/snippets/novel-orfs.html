Peptides placed outside annotated genes define candidate open reading frames in four classes: intergenic, out of frame inside a gene, antisense, and N-terminal extension. Novel peptides have a lower prior than annotated ones, so the error is estimated within each class and never pooled: database peptides compete with class-matched decoy ORFs, and de novo peptides use their genome decoy hits. An ORF is called when its class q-value is at most 0.01 by default and it meets its class rules. Without decoys, at least two distinct peptides are required and no FDR is given. For de novo ORFs the ORF q-value is the smallest peptide q-value, which can understate the ORF-level error.
