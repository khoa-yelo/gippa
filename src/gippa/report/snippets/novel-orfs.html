Peptides outside annotated genes define candidate open reading frames in seven classes. Novel peptides have a lower prior than annotated ones, so error is estimated within each class and never pooled. Database peptides compete with class-matched decoy ORFs, which exist for four classes (intergenic, out-of-frame in a gene, antisense, N-terminal extension); de novo peptides use their genome decoy hits in any class. An ORF is called when its class q-value is at most 0.01 by default and meets its class rules. When no class FDR can be estimated, an ORF needs at least two distinct peptides and carries no FDR. For de novo ORFs the ORF q-value is the smallest peptide q-value, which can understate ORF-level error.
