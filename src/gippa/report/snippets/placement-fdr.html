Each peptide is searched against the six-frame translation of the genome and, in the same way, against a decoy translation: reversed frames for database peptides, frames shuffled in windows of 50 residues for de novo peptides. The decoy has the size and composition of the target, so decoy hits estimate how many wrong peptides land in the genome by chance. Peptides are ranked by their expected number of chance hits, in separate lists for exact matches and for matches with one or two differences. Each list's decoy count gives the placement q-value; a placement is accepted when it is at most 0.01 by default.
